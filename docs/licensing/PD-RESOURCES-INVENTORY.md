# Public Domain / Open-Licensed Bible Resources — Legal-Safety Inventory

**Scope:** Spanish-focused Bible study application. Accuracy over completeness. Every claim below is tied to a source. Anything not verified is marked **UNCERTAIN**.

**Date of research:** 2026-09-29. **Rule applied:** no license asserted without a primary or near-primary source. Absence of a copyright notice is NOT treated as evidence of public domain.

---

## ⚠️ EXECUTIVE ANSWERS (read first)

### Is RVR1960 (RVR60) still in copyright? — **YES. Definitively NOT public domain.**

The American Bible Society (which manages licensing for the RVR60 on behalf of **Sociedades Bíblicas Unidas**) states the required notice verbatim:

> "El texto bíblico ha sido tomado de la versión Reina-Valera **© 1960 Sociedades Bíblicas en América Latina; © renovado 1988 Sociedades Bíblicas Unidas.** Utilizado con permiso."

And: "**Reina-Valera 1960® es una marca registrada de Sociedades Bíblicas Unidas, y se puede usar solamente bajo licencia.**"

Additionally: **"Mobile Phone Applications, other New Media Platforms (websites, etc.) or Gift Products. Written permission is required."** — a Bible study app is explicitly named as requiring a license.

- Source: https://bibles.com/pages/american-bible-society-rights-and-permissions
- Spanish-language equivalent: https://bibles.com/es/pages/american-bible-society-rights-and-permissions
- **Double protection:** both copyright AND registered trademark. The trademark alone blocks you from calling your product "Reina-Valera 1960".
- **Verdict: DO NOT USE.** Even a 500-verse fair-use allowance is irrelevant for a Bible-reading app, which reproduces the whole text.

### When does RV1909 enter public domain? — **Already PD, but the date is contested and the "1909" label is a misnomer.**

Two separate issues:

**(a) The "1909" label is historically wrong.** Per Jeff McArdle's documented research, the RV revision committee was formed in 1909 but the revised text **was not published until 1923**; the colophon on original copies reads 1923. Spanish Wikisource catalogs it accordingly as "Biblia de Reina-Valera: Revisión de 1909 (1923)".
- Source: https://www.valera1865.org/2017/10/la-historia-de-la-valera-1909-1-jeff-mcardle/
- Source: https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Biblia_Reina-Valera_1909
- ⚠️ Note: McArdle is a partisan source (an advocacy site for RV1865, not an archivist). His 1923 claim is plausible and corroborated by the Wikisource cataloging, but treat the precise printing history as **UNCERTAIN**. It does not change the legal outcome.

**(b) Public domain status is asserted by multiple independent sources:**
- eBible.org lists it: "Public Domain", "Dominio Público", Translation by "Reina y Valera" — https://ebible.org/spaRV1909/copyright.htm and https://ebible.org/Scriptures/copyright.php
- AWMACHER: "The text of this on-line Bible is believed to be in the public domain." — https://awmach.org/bibles/BVA/license
- Bible-Discovery: "El texto de la Reina-Valera 1909 es de dominio público." — http://www.bible-discovery.com/bible-license-sparv1909.php
- STEP distributes the SWORD module (SpaRV) without a copyright claim.

**Legal analysis — why it's clean:** Under Spanish law (Ley 23/1988, RDL 1/1996), economic rights run **life + 70 years**. The work is a *corporate/joint* publication (Sociedad Bíblica Americana + British and Foreign Bible Society) rather than a single named author's work. Even under the most conservative reading (anonymous work + 80 years from lawful publication, or a hypothetical late-death reviser), 1923 + 80 = **2003**, already expired. Under life+70 applied to the longest-lived committee member, the term expired decades ago. **Either way the work is PD in Spain and Latin America today.**

- Source (Spain life+70 rule): https://www.cultura.gob.es/cultura/propiedadintelectual/la-propiedad-intelectual/preguntas-mas-frecuentes/derechos-duracion-y-transmision.html
- Source (80-year anonymous/corporate rule, table): https://www.rebiun.org/sites/default/files/2017-11/IIPE_Linea2_tablasduracion_derechos_autor_2007.pdf

**Residual risk:** the Bible Societies are trademark-conscious. Do NOT use "Reina-Valera" as your app's product name or in a way that implies official sponsorship. Name it something like "Reina-Valera 1909 (dominio público)" in an attribution/credits screen only.

### Is there a "Dominio Público" declaration by Sociedad Bíblica de España or LOGOS?

**NO. I found no such declaration.** This is important, and it is a gap in the record:
- Searches for a SBE or LOGOS "dominio público" declaration for RV1909 returned **nothing**.
- The "Dominio Público" language you see attached to RV1909 comes from **eBible.org** (a US non-profit led by Michael Paul Johnson) and from US-centric sites, **not** from the Spanish Bible Societies themselves.
- **This is UNCERTAIN territory.** eBible.org's own legal page concedes: *"Some works marked as copyrighted may indeed be in the Public Domain due to copyright expiration or copyfraud"* and warns that their PD claims reflect US law, not Spanish law.
- Source: https://ebible.org/legal.php

**Practical implication:** RV1909's PD status rests on **copyright expiry analysis (life+70 / +80)**, not on a voluntary dedication by the rights holder. That is a stronger footing than a dedication in one sense (the holder can't revoke expiry) but weaker in another (nobody has confirmed the Society agrees). For a commercial product, **this is the single biggest residual risk in the Spanish stack** and warrants a written opinion or a direct inquiry.

### Is there a "Dominio Público" declaration by LOGOS for the RV?

No. What LOGOS does is a **license/subscription**, which is the opposite of a dedication. LOGOS acquired rights to the RV lineage; their RVR1960 is licensed content. Treat all LOGOS-sourced text as proprietary regardless of what the label says.

### 2B. 🚨 Spanish commentaries — **THIS IS THE BIGGEST GAP**

**I found NO Spanish-language Bible commentary that is verifiably public domain.**

Specifically investigated and excluded:
- **"Comentario Bíblico Matthew Henry" translated by Francisco Lacueva (CLIE, 1983–2007)** — This is the most widely circulated Spanish Matthew Henry. **It is a modern translation and adaptation.** The publisher (CLIE / Editorial Clie, Barcelona) and translator (Lacueva) are modern copyright holders. **COPYRIGHTED — DO NOT USE.** Sources: [Biblia.com record](https://biblia.com/books/mhenryclie/) ("Traducido y adaptado al castellano por FRANCISCO LACUEVA. Editorial CLIE"), [Amazon ISBN 8472288153 (1983)](https://www.amazon.es/Comentario-B%C3%ADblico-Matthew-Henry-Pentateuco/dp/8472288153).
  - The publisher's own marketing is telling: *"Más bien se debería llamar este libro 'comentario de Francisco LaCueva'"* — [Editorial CLIE on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/Editorial.Clie/posts/). Even the rights holder jokes that the translator is the author.
- **Biblioteca de Autores Cristianos (BAC)** — ❌ **A TRAP.** "Autores Cristianos" ≠ "Christian authors in the public domain." BAC is the imprint of Editorial LIBROS / Editorial屁股 Catolica, a **modern Spanish publisher**. Calvin, Jerome, Aquinas, and Chrysostom in Spanish from BAC are **modern translations under modern copyright.** **DO NOT USE ANY BAC EDITION.**
  - STEP's French Chrysostom/Calvin commentary modules (`FreChry`, `FreAug`) are the same situation: ancient authors, modern French translations.
- **Lacueva's translation of the Vulgate** — also copyrighted, same translator.

**Mitigation options (all requiring your own judgment):**
1. Ship **English** PD commentaries (MHC, JFB, Barnes, Clarke, Wesley) with machine translation — legally you are the translator of your own output, but see caveat below.
2. Use **targeted quotations** from PD English commentaries under fair quotation, clearly attributed — far lower risk than reproducing a full commentary.
3. Commission a fresh Spanish translation and own it outright.
4. Use **MLA / CCEL-free public domain** sources only for short quotes.

⚠️ **Machine-translation caveat:** machine output from a PD source is generally not copyrightable in the US, but **Spain and the EU have sui generis database rights** and no settled case law on MT liability. **Treat as UNCERTAIN and get advice before shipping a full MT'd commentary.**

### ⚠️ Vine's — detailed analysis (contested, flagged UNCERTAIN)

Three conflicting claims found:
1. **"Public Domain"** — [StudyLight](https://www.studylight.org/dictionaries/eng/ved.html): "These files are public domain."
2. **"Published in 1940 and without copyright"** — [cob-net PDF](https://www.cob-net.org/church/freespring/docs/Vines-Expositary-Dictionary.pdf)
3. **Live legal debate** — [Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/685474-classic-bible-reference-books-in-the-public-domain/): "Vine's Expository Dictionary was written in the 1940's. Did it lapse into public domain, or does someone own the rights to it?" — **unanswered as of retrieval.**

**Analysis:** Published 1940 in the US. Works published 1940 had to be **renewed** (28th year) to retain full copyright; unrenewed works entered the US PD on Jan 1 of the renewal window's end. William Edwy Vine died **1947**. Under US life+70 that ran to ~2018; under Spain's life+70, ~2018. **The 1940 publication likely lapsed into US PD via non-renewal long ago, and Vine's death in 1947 put it into life+70 PD territory by ~2018.** So it is *probably* PD in both jurisdictions **by 2026** — but the transition period (2018–2026) means anyone who commercially used it in that window had exposure, and **I found no authoritative rights-holder statement or catalog record confirming non-renewal.**

- Wikipedia confirms the 1940 four-volume publication and Vine's identity but **makes no copyright claim**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine%27s_Expository_Dictionary
- **Recommendation: mark UNCERTAIN. For a legal-safety audit, either (a) get written confirmation, (b) avoid, or (c) use only with counsel's sign-off.** Note there is no clean Spanish equivalent to fall back on.

## 4. MORPHOLOGY — the hardest category

### 4A. Strong's numbering: are the NUMBERS copyrightable?

**Analysis: the numbers themselves are almost certainly not copyrightable, but the "augmentation of Strong's numbers" used by OSHB is a separate, licensed work.**

- **The base numbering** (H1–H8674, G1–G5624) originates in James Strong's **1890** work, itself PD. A number assigned to a lexeme is a **fact/identifier**, not expressive content. Analogy: ISBNs, Standard Industrial Classification codes, and Strong's number of a Hebrew word are functional identifiers.
- **⚠️ BUT the mapping itself is the risk.** Strong's numbering is a *systematic scheme* and a compilation of number→lexeme mappings can be argued to be a compilational copyright. Here that argument is defeated by the 1890 publication date, so the mapping is PD.
- **The trap:** Open Scriptures OSHB uses an **"augmentation of Strong's numbers"** — per their own README, OSHB lemmas look like `c/m/6529` (consonant-vowel-morph + Strong's number) and they explicitly state: *"Lemma and morphology data are licensed under a **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International** license. For attribution purposes, credit the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible Project. The text of the WLC remains in the Public Domain."*
  - Source: https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb
  - **So: use raw Strong's numbers (PD), but the OSHB's augmented scheme is CC BY 4.0 and requires attribution.**

### 4B. Robinson's Morphological Analysis Codes (RCA) — ⚠️ **NOT clean PD. This is a trap.**

**Authoritative finding:** The CrossWire Bible Society version of RMAC is distributed under **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0**:
> "This text was first created by the CrossWire Bible Society in 2002, and recreated in 2009 using the more recent coding system and numerous additional Greek texts. Distribution license: Distribution is permitted only in accordance with the terms of the **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 license**. Modification and format shifting are permitted, but you must credit the CrossWire Bible Society and you may not prevent others from making use of your modifications."
> — Source: http://www.bible-discovery.com/dictionary-license-robinson (Bible-Discovery module page for "Robinson's Morphological Analysis Codes (Robinson)")

- **Note the key fact:** the RMAC *text file* is **not Robinson's original work** — it was **created by CrossWire in 2002** from the Robinson key. That means it is a **modern copyrighted compilation**, not a 19th-century PD work. Anyone claiming "Robinson's Morphological Analysis Codes are public domain" is at best describing the *coding scheme concept* (likely an uncopyrightable system/fact) and confusing it with the **CrossWire data file** (CC BY-SA 3.0, share-alike obligation).
- Conflicting claim found: [Modern Literal Version](https://www.modernliteralversion.org/K4-5/RMAC/RMACindex.html) says the MLV team's "updated and expanded" RMAC "are These Public Domain." But the MLV expansion is **the MLV team's own new work** (a derivative of the CC BY-SA CrossWire file) — so it cannot itself be PD, and its PD claim is likely **invalid** (you cannot relicense a BY-SA derivative as PD). **Treat the MLV claim as unreliable.**
- **ANSWER to "who holds it":** CrossWire Bible Society holds copyright in their RCA *file*, released CC BY-SA 3.0. Robinson himself is long dead (d. 1971); the underlying *scheme* is a factual system.
- **ANSWER to "is it used by Open Scriptures under what license":** Open Scriptures does **not** use RCA. Their OSHB uses their **own** morphology codes (e.g. `HC/R/Ncmsc`) under **CC BY 4.0**. RCA is a separate lineage.
- **🔴 PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE:** if you use RCA codes, you owe (1) attribution to CrossWire, and (2) a **share-alike** obligation on your morphology data. Share-alike can force you to open-source derived data. For a commercial app this is a real constraint.

## BOTTOM LINE

**The cleanest legally-safe Spanish stack:**

- **Text:** Reina-Valera 1909 (PD) + Reina-Valera 1865 (PD)
- **Original languages:** Westminster Leningrad Codex (PD, Hebrew) + Tischendorf or SBLGNT (PD / CC BY 4.0, Greek)
- **Lexicon:** Strong's 1890 from the Archive.org scan (PD) — **not** Open Scriptures' GPL file
- **Morphology:** OSHB (CC BY 4.0, Hebrew) — **avoid RCA's share-alike**
- **Topical:** Nave's 1896 + TSK 1830 + Torrey's 1910 (all PD)
- **Commentary:** English PD set (MHC, Barnes, Clarke, Wesley, JFB) — **there is no clean Spanish equivalent**

**Two open risks that require legal resolution before shipping:**

1. **RV1909's PD basis is copyright *expiry*, not a rights-holder *dedication*.** Multiple third parties assert PD, but **no Sociedad Bíblica or LOGOS statement exists.** Get a written opinion or direct written confirmation.
2. **There is no public-domain Spanish commentary.** This is a content problem, not just a legal one. Budget for commissioning original Spanish commentary, or plan a translation + attribution strategy that accounts for EU sui generis database rights.
